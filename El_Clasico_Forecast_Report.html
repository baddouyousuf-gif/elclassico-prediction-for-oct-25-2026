Bottom line

  • Barça are clear favourites but not runaway ones. The final blend is the form-and-minute model (65% / 19% / 16%), the betting markets (59% / 16% / 24%) and the pundits (54% / 24% / 22%). The model sits slightly more pro-Barça than the market.
  • Expect goals at both ends. Barça have scored in every match, and Madrid haven't kept a clean sheet in their last 12 Clásicos. The most likely scores are 2-1 (7.9%), 3-1 (7.4%), 3-2 (7.3%), 2-2 (7.1%), 1-1 (5.1%), 4-1 (5.0%).
  • The first goal is huge. If Barça score first, they win 77% of simulations. If Madrid score first, it's close to a coin flip: Barça 44%, draw 25%, Madrid 31%.
  • Madrid's path to a result: stay in the game until about 75 minutes, then use their late bench. 36% of their goals come after the 75th minute, and 6 of 33 came from substitutes (Espí 3, Endrick, Güler, Diomande). They need Barça's 46–60 burst to be survivable and Courtois at his best.
  • Barça's path: score early and keep scoring. Barça lead in 72% of their minutes this season, and when they concede they reply fast: median 10 minutes, and 12 of 15 goals conceded were answered within 15 minutes.

Who wins: every input and the blend

Weights: market 50%, form model 40%, pundits 10%. Markets get the most weight because they are the most efficient single input. The model is the independent view, the same idea as comparing a model PD with a CDS-implied PD. Market-implied expected goals are Barça 2.76, Madrid 1.77. The model's figures are Barça 2.98, Madrid 1.75, so the disagreement is almost entirely about how many Barça score.

How each team is playing right now

Barcelona (Flick): 9/9 in LaLiga, 50 goals in 12 games, first loss at PSG on Tuesday

  • Barça have 39 LaLiga goals, the second-best start ever, from 30.3 xG, while conceding 9 from 8.0 xGA. They average about 70% possession and play a very high line. Raphinha leads LaLiga with 12 league goals (14 in all competitions). Yamal has 8 league goals and 6 assists, and 14 goal involvements since 31 August, the most in Europe's top five leagues.
  • The weakness is the back four. Barça have used 10 different back lines in 12 games. Koundé, Eric García and Christensen are out, which leaves Cubarsí and Gerard Martín as the only senior centre-backs, with Cancelo and Espart at full-back. Goals have come from headers off crosses and set pieces (3), long shots (2, both at PSG) and individual errors (2). PSG scored at 81' and 86' to win 4-2, and Flick said: "We have to defend better than we did in Paris."
  • Rodri was kept on the bench at PSG with this game in mind. Olmo picked up an adductor injury in Paris (out about a month), so Fermín (5 goals) comes in. Yamal has reported some groin discomfort but is expected to play.
  • Barça have won 23 straight LaLiga home games and 6 of Flick's 7 Clásicos.

Real Madrid (Mourinho): 4 straight wins, 12 goals in the last 3

  • Madrid are 7W-2L in LaLiga (both defeats away, at Betis and Atlético) and 3/3 in the Champions League, beating Inter 2-1, Roma 4-3 from 3-1 down and Leipzig 4-1. Mbappé has 9 league goals (12 in all competitions) and has scored 12 in 10 games against Barça.
  • Shape: 4-2-3-1. Valverde has been used at right-back, with Tchouaméni and Bernardo (or Camavinga) as the pivot, Güler drifting in from the right, Bellingham at 10, Diomande wide left and Mbappé up front. The plan is to counter: unpredictable runs against Barça's high line, balls over the top for Mbappé, and Diomande attacking Espart's side.
  • Set pieces are a real weapon. At Roma, Madrid scored three times from corner sequences between 83' and 89'. Rüdiger has two late set-piece headers, and Güler takes most corners. Flick called Madrid "one of the best teams on set pieces."
  • Defensive concerns: no away clean sheet this season, and 5 of the 14 goals they've conceded came early in the second half (46–60'). They've also been reckless: Huijsen was sent off at Atlético and Vinícius against Villarreal.

The Vinícius situation

  • After the derby loss at Atlético (20 Sep), Mourinho benched Vinícius against Villarreal on 10 Oct. Mourinho said the aim was to "protect" him and "bring him on when the game called for a player like Vini." Vinícius came on at half-time and had a goal disallowed by VAR for pulling Nathan Saliba's hair, then got a straight red (about 73').
  • He got a 4-match LaLiga ban, 2 games for the incident plus 2 for protests, upheld on appeal. This is game 2 of the ban, so he's out until around 21 Nov (Celta). His season so far: 1 goal and 3 assists in 11 games, with fewer successful dribbles than usual and reports of a confidence dip after the World Cup.
  • Effect on the model: small (Madrid attack ×0.97). Diomande has 3 goals in his last 4 games, and Madrid beat Sevilla 4-1 with him starting on the left in Vinícius's place. Pundits like Troy Deeney see a selection headache coming when Vinícius returns.

Momentum: how long does a swing last?

  • Goals don't snowball, they get answered. Across all 24 matches, when two goals came within 10 minutes of each other, the second went to the same team 55% of the time, below the 62% you'd expect from scoring rates alone. So the model applies no "hot streak" bonus (momentum multiplier = 1.0).
  • Barça bounce back fast: 13 of 15 goals conceded were answered, 12 of them within 15 minutes (median 10').
  • Madrid bounce back slowly but late: 12 of 14 goals conceded were answered, but only 4 within 15 minutes (median 22.5'). Their replies arrive when the substitutes come on.

Did last season improve the model?

Each option was tested with leave-one-out cross-validation: each of the 24 matches this season is predicted using only the other 23, and the Poisson log-likelihood of the actual goals is scored (higher is better). A prior is kept only if it improves that score.

  • Flick's 2025-26 Barcelona (95 scored, 36 conceded in 38): rejected. Every amount of prior weight made predictions worse, because this season's Barça are better than last season's. Barça are rated on this season only.
  • Mourinho's 2025-26 Benfica (74 scored, 25 conceded in 34, unbeaten league season, compact 5-3-2 block against strong sides): accepted (+0.65 log-likelihood). Mourinho's defensive identity is predictive of how this Madrid side performs. The test's best weight was 38 games, but I used the most conservative weight within 0.05 of the best (12 games), because it comes from a weaker league.
  • The test also found that Madrid's most recent games are more predictive (recency half-life 4 matches), while Barça's form is steady (half-life 8).

Model method

  1. Form ratings: opponent-adjusted attack and defence from all 24 LaLiga and UCL matches. LaLiga opponents are rated from the current table (with the match itself removed and the ratings shrunk 50%), and UCL opponents are rated by judgment. Home ×1.15, away ×0.87, with time-decay weighting.
  2. xG correction: goals are pulled toward xG (60% weight). Barça are scoring above their xG (39 vs 30.3), and Madrid have conceded below theirs (9 vs 10.6 xGA).
  3. Last-season priors: only where cross-validation shows an improvement (see above).
  4. Availability shocks: Barça defence ×1.06 (three centre-backs out), Barça attack ×0.98 (Olmo, Yamal doubt), Madrid attack ×0.97 (Vinícius), Madrid defence ×1.01 (Mendy, Asencio).
  5. Minute engine: team-specific 15-minute scoring and conceding profiles (shrunk toward a league-typical profile), game-state multipliers, momentum test, red-card risk and stoppage time, simulated 100,000 times.
  6. Ensemble with the de-vigged market consensus and pundit sentiment.

Backtest: the rating model's leave-one-out Brier score is 0.209, against 0.496 for a naive base-rate forecast. This mainly shows it separates strong from weak opponents. Calibration in elite-vs-elite games is less certain, which is why the market gets the largest weight.

Caveats

  • The sample is 12 matches per team. Some goal minutes (Racing, Sevilla, Getafe) and some substitution times are approximate and flagged in the data files.
  • UCL opponent strengths, home advantage and the availability shocks are judgment calls. The sensitivity table shows how much each one matters.
  • Team news can change on the day. Re-run with the confirmed XIs: edit SHOCKS in model/clasico_model_v2.py, then run the model, charts.py and build_report.py.
  • This is a probabilistic forecast, not betting advice.
